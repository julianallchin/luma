# Color

Color is the emotional register of the show. Brightness says how much; color
says what kind. The most common amateur failure is treating color as variety —
cycling hues because more feels like more. It reads as noise. A held color is
a statement; a cycling rainbow is a screensaver.

## The rules that hold

- **A section is a palette.** Two colors, maybe three with one as accent-only.
  Choose them before authoring the section, name what each is for, and hold
  them for the whole block. Palette changes ARE section changes — spending one
  mid-block spends structure you don't get back.
- **Contrast comes from relationships, not hue count.** Warm against cool
  (amber/blue), saturated against desaturated, one hue against darkness. Two
  well-opposed colors out-dramatize six random ones every time.
- **Temperature is the arc.** Cold → warm across a build is a lift the body
  feels. Warm verse, cold drop is betrayal (the good kind). Track temperature
  across the whole show the way you track brightness terracing.
- **White is a color, and it's expensive.** Full white reads as revelation or
  blast — the biggest single accent available. More than a few moments of it
  and it's a work light.
- **Red is violence, use it like violence.** The face-punch drop, the menace
  section. A track that's been blue and amber for three minutes turning full
  red is an event. Red all night is a brake light.

## Meanings you can lean on

Not laws — defaults the audience already carries:

- Amber/gold: warmth, soul, human. Home for house, funk, anything organic.
- Blue: cool, deep, night. The safest foundation in electronic music.
- Purple/violet: lush, expensive, melodic. Future bass and trance love it.
- Red: aggression, danger, peak. Reserve it.
- Green: acid, toxic, machine. Rare — which makes it potent in techno.
- Pink/magenta: playful, sensual, modern. Disco, pop, melodic house.
- White: impact, truth, arrival.
- UV-deep-blue near black: anticipation. The pre-drop bar's best friend.

## Harmony can drive hue

When the music is melodic, let the chords steer: map chord changes
(`features.chords`) to hue moves within the palette — not a new color per
chord, but a lean (blue toward violet on the minor lift, amber toward gold on
resolve). The room won't name it; they'll feel the light agreeing with the
song. Trance, melodic house, and melodic bass earn this; percussive genres
don't — there, color belongs to sections.

## Choosing a palette for a track

Ask three questions: what temperature is this music (organic-warm or
machine-cold)? what's its one accent moment and what color is that? what
should the room look like in a phone photo? Answer those, pick two colors and
one accent, and you're done — the discipline is staying done.
