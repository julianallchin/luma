# Melodic bass

Same halftime bones as heavy bass — snare on 3, felt tempo half the BPM — but
the soul is opposite. The bass is a warm ocean, the chords are the event, and
the drop *blooms* instead of punching. If you find yourself planning strobes,
re-listen; if the snare is artillery and the bass is snarling, you're in
`heavy-bass` territory.

## The one idea

Light the swells. This music is built from chords that inflate and release —
sidechained pads breathing against the kick, supersaws opening up, vocal chops
riding the top. Your job is to make the room inhale and exhale with them.

- Slow attacks, long releases. Envelopes here open over a beat or two and take
  their time falling. Nothing snaps unless the music snaps.
- Color does the melody's work. Chord changes (from `features.chords`) are hue
  changes; a lift into the drop is a lift in temperature or saturation, not
  just brightness.
- Motion travels. Waves that cross the rig over a phrase, blooms that start at
  one point and spread — movement should flow through space the way the sound
  pans and swells, not tick in place.

## Feel

- The drop is the *widest* moment, not the hardest. Everything on, but soft
  edges — full rig at a warm sustain beats full rig strobing.
- Vocal chops are sparkle. Small bright accents on a secondary group, following
  the chop rhythm loosely — decoration, not structure.
- The bass still moves (measure it — toolbox recipe). A slow 1–2 Hz undulation
  under the drop wants a matching brightness breathe on the foundation. Deep
  and flowing, never gated on/off.

## The experimental license

This is the genre for the weird pattern you've been wanting to justify — but
slow weird, not fast weird. Unusual spatial shapes, strange color pairs,
a pattern running at quarter speed across the whole rig. The audience is
floating; give them something to float on. Rules that keep it musical:

- One experiment per section. Anchored by a conventional foundation.
- The experiment breathes at phrase length. If it cycles faster than a bar it
  reads as glitch, not dream.
- If it doesn't clearly follow something audible — a swell, a chord rhythm,
  a vocal line — it's decoration noise. Cut it.

## Structure

Phrases are long and the arc is smooth. Terrace gently: intro low and warm,
build inflates, drop wide, second drop widest or strip it to the vocal and
rebuild. Breakdowns can go very low — one dim color, one slow motion — because
this genre's breakdowns are the emotional center, not a pause. The a cappella
or piano moment, if the track has one, is the most valuable bar in the song:
near-dark, one color, stillness.

## How deep to go

Moderate. Per-phrase, not per-onset. The swell map (chord changes + envelope
peaks per phrase) is usually enough to score the whole track in one pass, and
one pass keeps the flow coherent — this genre suffers most when sections feel
authored by different hands. Fan out only when the track is genuinely too big
for one careful pass, along its own natural seams.
