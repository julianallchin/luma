This page defines the terms that the Luma code and documentation use.

## Data Overview

```
Library                                  Venue
|-- Tracks                               |-- Fixtures (universe, address, mode, position)
|   |-- Beat grid                        |-- Groups
|   |-- Stems (drums, bass, vocals,      |-- Stage pieces
|   |   other)                           |-- Folders (links to tracks)
|   |                                    |-- Laser zones
|   |                                    |-- Perform set, layout, controllers
|   |                                    '-- Members
|   |-- Roots (chord analysis)
|   |-- Drum onsets
|   '-- Bar classifications, genres
'-- Scores (one track + one venue)
    '-- Clips (name, graph, start, duration, z index, blend mode, selection, seed)
```

## Mode

One of the three ways a window shows its venue: **Rig** (the stage builder, patch and groups), **Arrange** (one score's timeline) and **Live** (the decks and the Perform set). See [The Window](/docs/user-guide/the-window).

## Venue

A physical room. A venue holds its fixtures, their DMX addresses, their positions, its stage pieces and its groups. A venue also records its environment (indoor or outdoor) and its haze.

## Fixture

One light that is patched into a venue. A fixture has a fixture definition, a mode, a DMX universe, a start address, a position and a rotation. Positions use a Z-up, right-handed coordinate system in metres.

## Head

One part of a fixture that Luma controls on its own. A pixel bar with 12 cells has 12 heads. Output state is keyed by `fixture-id` or `fixture-id:head-index`.

## DMX Universe

A set of 512 DMX channels. A venue can use more than one universe.

## Group

A named set of fixtures in one venue. A score selects fixtures by group name, not by fixture id. Group names are snake_case. They must match `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*` and cannot be `all`. See [Groups](/docs/user-guide/groups-and-tags).

## Selection Expression

A query over group names. It uses `&` (and), `|` (or), `^` (either side, chosen at random when both match), `~` (not), `>` (fallback) and parentheses. An empty expression or `all` selects every fixture.

## Track

An audio file in the library, with its analysis: beat grid, stems, chord roots, drum onsets, bar classifications and genres.

## Score

The light show for one track in one venue. A score is stored as rows: one `scores` row and one `clips` row per clip. A track can have many scores.

## Clip

One placement of a clip graph on a score's timeline. A clip has a name, a start and a duration in beats, a z index, a blend mode, a selection expression, a seed and its own graph.

## Clip Graph

The small graph that a clip owns. It has 1 to 64 nodes of 19 kinds, and exactly one of them is the output node. See the [node reference](/docs/node-reference).

## Output Node

The node of a clip graph that says what the clip does: `color` (color, brightness, alpha), `aim` (where moving heads point), `strobe` (rate, alpha) or `laser` (figures on laser projectors). A clip writes only what its output node drives.

## Preset

A shipped clip graph with a name and a blend mode, such as Chase or Rainbow. Adding a preset copies its graph into the new clip. See [Clips and Presets](/docs/user-guide/patterns).

## Perform Set

A venue's live patterns: clip graphs with no timeline, laid out on pages of a grid and played from pads, faders and controllers in Live mode. See [Live](/docs/user-guide/performing).

## Controller

A MIDI device a venue knows by its port name, with an optional profile that says where its pads and faders sit and how to light them. A **binding** maps one of its inputs, or an OSC address, to a live control.

## Laser Zone

A named window of directions in a venue, shared by every laser projector. A laser clip draws on a canvas from −1 to 1 that maps onto the zone. See [Lasers](/docs/user-guide/lasers).

## Kernel Graph

The internal graph a clip graph lowers onto before it plays: primitive kernels from `luma_patterns::standard_library()`, wired together. Users and agents never edit it.

## Signal

The value that flows on a kernel graph's wire. A signal is a three-dimensional array (heads × time × channels) with a unit and a channel layout.

## Blend Mode

How a clip combines with the clips below it. Light has seven modes; see the [blend mode table](/docs/architecture/compositor#blend-modes). An aim clip takes **replace** or **offset**: offset turns the aim under the clip by the aim node's yaw and pitch. A laser clip takes **replace** or **add**.

## PrimitiveState

The output of one head at one moment: dimmer (0–1), color (linear Rec. 2020, 0–1), strobe (0–1), position (pan and tilt in degrees), speed (0 or 1) and aim (a direction in the room and a weight, which a solver turns into pan and tilt).

## UniverseState

The `PrimitiveState` of every head at one moment. It is not a DMX universe.
