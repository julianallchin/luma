# Place Clips

In **Arrange** mode (key `2`), the track editor shows the open score's track on a timeline under the stage. You place patterns on the timeline as **clips**. The clips of one track in one venue make a **score**.

## Scores

A score belongs to one track and one venue. A track can have many scores. The song list's third column (⌘P, see [The Window](/docs/user-guide/the-window#the-song-list)) shows a song's scores: this venue's first, with **New score**, then each other venue's under that venue's name. Each row shows the score's author, its clip count and its age. A click opens a score. Double-click the name of one of this venue's scores to rename it, or right-click it to delete it.

The menu at the top of the stage names the open song and score. It lists the song's other scores too. Opening a score from another venue asks first, then switches the window to that venue.

Anyone with access to the venue can edit any of its scores, whoever wrote them. The row's author says who wrote it.

## Play

The strip above the timeline starts with the transport: play or pause, the position and the tempo. **Space** plays and pauses, and the ruler scrubs. **F** keeps the view on the playhead, and **H** fits every lane. **⌘L** loops the selected span. The [shortcuts table](/docs/user-guide/settings#shortcuts) lists the editing keys: split, copy, cut, paste, duplicate, delete, undo and moving clips between lanes.

## Add a Clip

1. Click **Add pattern**, or right-click the timeline. The **Insert pattern** dialog opens.
2. The dialog lists the presets and a blank start for each output. It shows a looping preview of the highlighted preset on the current venue.
3. Choose one. Luma adds it to the timeline as a clip.

See [Clips and Presets](/docs/user-guide/patterns) for what a clip plays.

## Clip Properties

| Property | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Name** | The clip's name. The timeline shows it with a one-line summary of the graph. |
| **Start** | Where the clip begins, in beats. |
| **Duration** | How long the clip lasts, in beats. |
| **Z index** | The stacking order. A clip with a higher z index is placed above a clip with a lower z index. |
| **Blend mode** | How the clip combines with the clips below it. The default is `replace`. |
| **Selection** | A [selection](/docs/user-guide/groups-and-tags#selections): every fixture, or one or more groups. The default is `all`. |
| **Graph** | The clip's own [graph](/docs/user-guide/patterns#the-graph) of nodes. |
| **Seed** | The seed for the graph's random choices. |

Double-click a clip to open its graph page. It shows the clip's name, selection, blend mode and graph.

**Pick fixtures** opens a dialog. It lists the venue's groups next to a picture of the venue. Tick groups to select their union, such as `front_wash | back_movers`. With nothing ticked, the clip selects every fixture. If the selection has a group that was deleted, the dialog says so. Tick a group to replace it.

## How Clips Combine

Luma evaluates the score for each frame. It takes the clips that are active at that time and folds them from the lowest z index to the highest.

For each head:

- **Only the capabilities that a clip drives** take part. A color clip does not change strobe or aim.
- **Color and dimmer** blend with the clip's blend mode, then mix with the light below by the clip's alpha. Alpha 0 leaves the light below as it was, in every mode. Where no clip lit the head, the light below is no light.
- **Strobe** blends the same way, against no strobe.
- **Aim** blends by the aim clip's blend mode, below. After the clips are combined, Luma turns each head's aim into pan and tilt.

The seven light blend modes and their formulas are in the [blend mode table](/docs/architecture/compositor#blend-modes). An aim clip takes one of two modes instead. **Replace** turns the heads toward the clip's aim along the shortest arc, by its alpha. **Offset** turns the aim under it by the clip's yaw and pitch, times its alpha. Over no aim, it starts from the head's home. Offset clips stack, so one movement clip can run over many positions. A laser clip takes **Replace**, which morphs the elements under it into its own, or **Add**, which draws both.
