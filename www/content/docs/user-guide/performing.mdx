# Live

**Live** is the third mode of a venue's window (key `3`, or **Live** in the
title bar's mode switch). It holds the deck panel beside the stage and the
venue's **Perform set** under it. Switching to another mode stops nothing:
Luma keeps following the decks and keeps every value.

![Live mode: the deck panel playing a song on the virtual deck beside the stage, and a page of pads for washes, chases, laser looks and strobes under them.](/screenshots/live.webp)

## The Deck Panel

The deck panel sits at the left of the stage. Drag its edge to resize it. It
shows each deck Luma follows: what is loaded, the bar and beat, and a
scrolling waveform with the playhead in the middle. With no deck to follow,
the set plays on a tapped tempo.

Under the decks is the DJ source. **Add source** lists the sources and the
latency. Once a source is connected you can change it or disconnect it, which
puts the set back on the tapped tempo. The MIDI and OSC status sits under it,
and the status bar says what is followed.

| Source | Hardware | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Rekordbox** | rekordbox on this computer | Read through [rkbx_link](https://github.com/grufkork/rkbx_link) over OSC. Turn on `osc.msg.n/time` in rkbx_link's config; it is off by default. Load tracks after Luma starts listening. |
| **Pro DJ Link** | Pioneer CDJs | To connect, Luma claims a virtual player number. That number must not be the number of a real deck on the network. |
| **StageLinQ** | Denon DJ / Engine DJ | Luma listens for devices and reads deck telemetry. |
| **Virtual** | None | Luma's own player is the deck. **Choose song** picks any library track, which loads and plays. Use it to practise and to see patterns. |

Luma matches the track each deck loads against the library and plays that
track's scores for the venue. Each deck renders its own layer at its own time,
and the layers blend by deck volume. Between reports from the source, Luma
runs each deck's clock forward, so the lights keep moving.

## The Perform Set

Each venue has a set of live patterns. A pattern is a clip graph with no
timeline: a selection, a blend mode and a graph. The set is laid out on pages
of a grid, like a console's executor window. The page tabs run along the top;
**Edit layout** at the end of their row arranges the grid, and **Done** goes
back to playing.

A cell holds one of:

- **A pattern.** Its pad turns it on and off. The cell also has the
  pattern's level and a fader or button for each fader and button node in its
  graph.
- **A group.** Tap it to choose that group. It stays chosen until you tap it
  again.
- **A list** of colors or numbers. A tap sets the next value. A list can step
  on by itself every few bars.
- **A fader** that moves several things together, such as three chases'
  speeds.
- **Select**, **Clear**, **tap** tempo or the **master**.

### Pads

A pattern's pad is a `button` node wired into its output's alpha. A pad plays
as **toggle** (on until the next press) or **flash** (on while held). It fades
with its own attack, decay, sustain and release. A press can land at once, or
on the next beat or bar of the set's clock; a pad shows that it is waiting.

Every pattern plays at its level times the master, over the decks. In a row
whose patterns play one at a time, a pattern that comes on releases the
others.

### Choose Where, Then Act

Tap a group, then a pad: the pattern plays on that group. Hold a group while
you press a pad to act on that group for one press only. With nothing chosen,
a pad plays its pattern on its own selection.

A color list with no targets sets the **base color**: the color under every
effect, on the chosen groups or on every light. A pattern with the **Below**
blend takes the color already under it. Hold **Select** (or tap it to latch
it) and press pads to choose patterns instead of playing them; the next color
you tap becomes their own color. Tap **Clear** to choose nothing. Hold it for
half a second to let every effect go.

### Edit Layout

In **Edit layout**, click a **+** to put something in that place: a new
pattern from a preset, a group, a list of colors or numbers, a fader, the
master, tap, Select or Clear. Drag cells to move and resize them. Turn on the switch
at a row's left to make its patterns play one at a time. The inspector beside
the grid edits the selected cell, or the page when no cell is selected: its
name, its columns and rows, and its controller.

- **Edit pattern…** opens the pattern's graph, the same editor a score's clips
  use. See [Clips and Presets](/docs/user-guide/patterns).
- **+** beside the page tabs adds a page.
- **Busk page** builds a whole page from the rig: group cells, base and accent
  color lists, effects from the presets with their own pads and speed faders,
  movement when the rig has movers, hits such as strobe and blinder, and
  Select, Clear, tap and the master. It never names a fixture, so it works on
  a small rig and a large one.

Live has its own chat in the agent panel. Ask it to add, change or lay out
patterns.

## MIDI and OSC

Any MIDI device and anything that sends OSC (to UDP port 8000) can drive a
fader or a pad. In Edit layout, select a cell and press **Learn** beside one of
its controls, then move the fader or press the key. A binding says what the
hardware sends: a fader's position, a pad's press and release, or a
**latching** key. It can be inverted. Learning an input again changes its
binding.

Bindings belong to a **controller**, and controllers and bindings sync with
the venue. A controller with a profile knows where its pads and faders sit,
and its pad lights show what each control does: an effect dim while off and
bright while on, blinking while it waits for its beat. **Map this page** puts
the page's cells on its grid. Luma ships no profiles: press **Set up with the
agent** in the inspector's Controller section, and the chat writes one with
you, lighting pads one at a time and asking what you see. Click the
controller's name to rename it.

## DMX Output

Luma sends DMX to Art-Net nodes and to USB DMX dongles. Turn it on in
**Settings → DMX Output**:

| Setting | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Max Brightness** | Limits the brightness of all DMX output. 100 means no limit. |
| **Enable DMX Output** | Turns every output on or off. While it is off, Luma holds no socket or device. |
| **Art-Net Interface IP** | The address Art-Net binds. `0.0.0.0` binds to all interfaces. It cannot be edited in the app yet. |

Choose where each universe goes under **Outputs** on the venue page in Rig. A
universe can go to several ports. A universe with no output is not sent.
Luma finds Art-Net nodes on the network by ArtPoll and lists the ports each
one reports. The supported USB dongles are the SoundSwitch Micro DMX
Interface and the SoundSwitch Control One. See the
[DMX pipeline](/docs/architecture/dmx-pipeline) for the details.

## From Values to DMX

1. Luma evaluates the active clips and [combines them](/docs/user-guide/annotations#how-clips-combine) into one `UniverseState`.
2. It maps each head's dimmer, color, strobe, position and speed to the DMX channels of the fixture's mode.
3. It writes one DMX buffer for each universe and hands it to each output that the universe is routed to.
