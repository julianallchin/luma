# Define Your Venue

A venue is one physical room. It holds the fixtures in the room, their DMX addresses, their positions, the stage pieces and the groups.

## Rig

A window shows one venue. Its **Rig** mode (key `1`) is where you build it: the stage at the top, and the venue page under it with the patch and the groups. See [The Window](/docs/user-guide/the-window).

![Rig mode: a venue's laser projectors and LED bars on truss towers, the environment and haze controls beside the 3D view, and the patch and groups under it.](/screenshots/rig.webp)

## Patch Fixtures

The patch table lists each fixture with its **Model**, **Mode**, **Universe**, **Address**, the **Channels** it uses and its **Groups**.

You add lights on the stage, not in the table. Every light has a place on the stage. When you place a light, it gets a row in the patch table. When you delete a light, its row goes too.

1. Choose the **Mode**. The mode sets how many DMX channels the fixture uses and what each channel does. Choose the mode that is set on the real fixture.
2. Set the **Universe** and **Address**, or let Luma pick them. **Auto patch** assigns addresses for you. It addresses each truss in order along its length and packs the trusses one after another, so a rig that fits in one universe uses one universe.

The backend assigns every address. The patch page shows an error when the backend refuses an address. **Auto patch** keeps the addresses you set by hand and packs the other fixtures around them.

Switch the page from **Fixtures** to **Patch** to see each universe as a grid of 512 cells, one for each channel. Each fixture fills as many cells as it has channels. Drag a fixture to give it a new address, or onto another universe to move it there. **Output** lists each universe, how full it is and where it is sent, beside the DMX devices this computer can see.

A universe has 512 channels. A fixture that uses 16 channels and starts at address 33 uses channels 33 to 48.

## Send DMX

The **Outputs** chip lists each universe and where it goes. **Add output** sends a universe to a port of an Art-Net node or a USB DMX dongle that Luma found; **Unbind** takes it off. A universe can go to more than one port. A universe with no output is not sent anywhere. Turn output on in **Settings → DMX Output**. See [Live](/docs/user-guide/performing#dmx-output).

## Build the Stage

The stage in Rig is the only place where you set positions.

- Press **A**, or **Add**, to open the add dialog. It offers **Fixtures** or **Stage pieces**; **Tab** switches between them, and the preview shows the one under the cursor.
- Choose a piece, such as a truss, and place it.
- Choose a fixture and click a piece, such as a truss, to lay a row along it. Click the floor to stand one light there; click again for the next. The fixtures come from the Luma fixture library.
- A light on the floor moves with the gizmo like a piece does. A light on a truss moves with its row.
- **W** and **E** switch the gizmo between move and rotate. **⌘D** duplicates the selection. **F** frames it.
- Delete a piece, and the lights on it are deleted too. **⌘Z** brings them back.
- A piece snaps to a socket first, then to the surface under the cursor, then to the ground grid.

Positions use a Z-up, right-handed coordinate system in metres.

### Why Positions Matter

A clip graph can read head positions: a `space` node gives each head its place along a direction, by its order, or around a centre. A chase can travel from one side of a group to the other. Because graphs read positions, the same clip works with 8 fixtures or 80.

## Multi-Head Fixtures

Some fixtures have many parts that Luma controls one by one, such as the cells of a pixel bar. Luma calls each part a **head**. Output state is keyed by `fixture-id:head-index`. Graphs run per head, so spatial effects work at the level of each cell.

## View the Rig

The visualizer shows the venue in 3D. The camera button opens **View settings**:

| Setting | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Environment** | Indoor or outdoor |
| **Haze** | Turns volumetric haze on or off |
| **Haze density** | How thick the haze is |
| **Cloudiness** | How uneven the haze is (0–1) |
| **Cloud size (m)** | Size of the haze clouds |
| **Turbulence** | How much the haze moves (0–1) |
| **Wind speed (m/s)** | Speed of the haze drift |
| **Wind direction (°)** | Direction of the haze drift |
| **Grid** | Shows the ground grid |
| **Gizmos** | Shows gizmos |
| **Render scale (%)** | Render resolution |

The environment and the haze belong to the venue. They sync with the venue to your other devices. The grid, gizmos and render scale belong to this device.

## Venue Members

A venue has an owner and can have members. Venue content syncs to the owner and to each member.

- **Share** in the title bar's venue menu opens the venue's invite code. **Create invite code** makes one. **Copy code**, **New code** and **Revoke** are beside it. Anyone with the code can open and edit the venue.
- The same page lists the people with access. The owner can remove a member.
- **Join with code…** in the venue menu joins someone else's venue. Its content then downloads like any other synced rows. The venue menu marks it **Shared with you**.
