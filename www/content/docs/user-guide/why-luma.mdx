# Why Luma Exists

A traditional lighting console records DMX values for specific hardware. For example: "set channel 47 to 200". Channel 47 means something only in one room with one rig. In a different venue, the show does not work. You must program it again.

This is a problem for:

- **DJs and small artists.** They play in many rooms and cannot program each rig.
- **Touring shows.** A lighting designer must program each venue again.
- **Small events.** They often have no programmed lighting.

## Luma's Approach

Luma records what the light does, not which channel it sets. A clip says "pulse a color on `front_wash` on every fourth beat". Each venue decides which fixtures are in `front_wash`.

## The Two Parts

### Your Library

Tracks, scores, clips and pattern graphs. This data never names a DMX channel or a fixture id. It syncs to your other devices when you are signed in. You must be signed in to make changes.

### Venues

A venue records the fixtures in one room, their addresses, their positions and their groups. You set up a venue once. Then every score for that venue plays in that room.

## The Workflow

1. **[Define your venue](/docs/user-guide/venues).** Patch fixtures and build the stage.
2. **[Create groups](/docs/user-guide/groups-and-tags).** Name the sets of fixtures that scores will target.
3. **[Import tracks](/docs/user-guide/importing-tracks).** Luma analyzes the audio.
4. **[Choose forms](/docs/user-guide/patterns).** Pick a preset and set its inputs.
5. **[Place clips](/docs/user-guide/annotations).** Put patterns on a track's timeline.
6. **[Perform](/docs/user-guide/performing).** Not yet available in the native app.
