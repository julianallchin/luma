Execute Python in a namespace that persists for this agent thread. Current Luma state lives under `luma` and is refreshed before every call; the variables, functions and imports you create persist across calls. numpy, scipy, librosa and matplotlib are available. You get back stdout, stderr, the last expression's value, a traceback when it fails, and any matplotlib figures as images you can actually see. Write normal cell-shaped Python — no wrapper function, no `return`.

For every call, name the cell's work before its code. Choose a verb that fits the work, and give it in two forms: `verb` ends in -ing and `verbPast` is its past tense, for example “Measuring” and “Measured”. `purpose` is a short phrase that follows the verb, for example “beat energy in bars 7–13”. Do not restate the code.

Orientation:
- `luma.catalog()` gives a compact overview. Drill into a relevant path, such as `luma.catalog("venue")` or `luma.catalog("features.drum_onsets")`; use `depth=` to see more of that branch. Request `depth=None` only when you need its full inventory.
- `edit = luma.track.edit()` captures the score document. A clip has a name, a time range, a selection, a blend mode, a seed and one graph. The builders are bare names in every cell: `time`, `space`, `along`, `random`, `audio`, `curve`, `mirror`, `shuffle`, `group`, `split`, `figure`, `max`, `min`, and the outputs `color`, `aim`, `strobe`, `laser`; `preset("Chase")` copies a shipped clip. Coordinates (`time`, `space`, `random`, `audio`) go through `curve` into an input: `color(brightness=curve(time(every=1), "Drop"))`. Curves combine with `*`, `+`, `-`, `max()` and `min()`: `brightness=cut * fade` (a list is an error). `alpha` on `color` and `strobe` is the clip's opacity. Values are plain: numbers, `(u, v, z)`, `(r, g, b)` or `"#RRGGBB"`, curve and gradient preset names. Random gives a value, Shuffle gives an order. The same variable wired twice is one node. Place with `edit.add_clip(graph, name="Pulse", at=("9", "13"), selection="front_wash")`, or use `bars=(9, 13)` or `seconds=(start, end)`. A position is `bar.beat.16th`, all three from 1, as in the editor; `"9"` is `9.1.1`. Every range includes its start and excludes its end. `clip.at` gives a clip's range as positions. `luma.track.position_at(seconds)` and `luma.track.seconds_at(position)` convert between time and position. A tuple color is linear Rec. 2020; `luma.track.from_srgb("#ff8000")` converts sRGB. `add_clip` and `edit.update_clip(clip, graph=..., name=...)` check that clip at once and raise `ClipError` with the node, the input and an example fix. `edit.remove_clip(clip)` deletes it. `clip.graph.source()` prints Python that rebuilds a clip's graph. A selection always lights its whole group.
- Layers combine clips through blend modes. `edit.source()` is the canonical score JSON; `edit.replace_source(source)` stages it. Native editing, Python and model-authored JSON use one checker.
- Verify with `edit.diff()`, `edit.check()` and `view = edit.window(...)`. `edit.diff()` prints a short plan, one line per changed clip; `edit.diff(full=True)` gives the raw clips. `view.timeline()` plots clips; `view.output.heatmap()` renders the actual candidate; `view.output.values` is `[primitive, time, rgb]`, `view.output.aim.values` / `.aim.weight` the aim and `view.output.strobe.values` the shutter. Inspect a proposed clip in 3D with `luma.venue.render(edit=edit, only=clip, t=...)`, then omit `only` to inspect the full draft and its blends at representative timestamps. `edit.apply()` writes only the clips the edit added, changed or removed; a clip saved elsewhere meanwhile keeps that version unless this edit touched it too. Open the edit right before you start changing things and apply promptly. In a child workspace it advances only the detached score, which a supervisor later merges.
- `luma.scores` reads your other scores: `find(text=None, track=None)` lists them, this song's first; `open(found[0])` gives a read-only reference with `looks()`, `roles()`, `look(name)`, `clips`, `source()` and `venue` (`describe()`, `groups()`). Its positions are this track's when it is the same song.
- `luma.music` is the track as heard. `luma.music.feel` gives the felt tempo against the grid (often 2x). `listen(bars)`, `deviations(bars)`, `modulation(bars)`, `similar(at)` and `sections()` return compact text; `onsets`, `mert`, `envelopes` and `beat_features` hold the arrays behind them. `find_like(at, length=None, end=None, stem="mix")` finds every moment that sounds like a span: `at` is a position or range (`"13.2.3"`, `"13.2.3-13.3.1"`) or seconds, `length` counts felt 16ths, `end` is a position or seconds; `stem` is `"mix"` or `"drums"` (the stems with a MERT cache). It returns a score over time, not matches: `time_s` and `score` at every 75 Hz MERT frame, where `score[t]` is the cosine between the query and the same-length window starting at `t`, both mean-pooled MERT layer 7 frames; `.sixteenths` is the same score on the felt 16th grid (the max of the frames nearest each 16th, with `at`, `bar`, `beat`, `sixteenth`, `slot`). It is a raw cosine, not calibrated across tracks or stems; the query itself scores 1 and its neighbours score high. It picks nothing: you choose the peaks, the threshold and the snapping. Its positions are `bar.beat.16th` too; quote them as printed. At 2x feel a felt 16th is half a 16th, such as `13.2.3.5`. n2n hat onsets are low-confidence.
- `luma.audio` is signal: `mix`, the demucs stems `vocals`, `drums`, `bass` and `other` (synths, keys, pads and everything else), and `rest` (the mix without vocals). Stems are demucs estimates, not multitracks: a sound can bleed across them, so a stem tells you where a sound mostly sits, not that it is absent from the others. `luma.features` is what was derived from audio (beats, downbeats, drum onsets, bar classifications, chords, waveform bands, MERT). Pick the branch that matches the question.
- Tensors expose `.values` (numpy), `.shape`, `.axes`, `.times_s`, `.unit`, `.provenance`. Keyed families are dict-style: `luma.features.drum_onsets["kick"]`, `luma.music.onsets["kick"]`.
- `luma.venue` is the room. Plan coordinates are `u` (stage right), `v` (toward the audience), and height `z`, in metres; angles are degrees. Use `describe()` for a compact live overview and `nodes()` for placed geometry. `fixtures` is the patch; `fixture_library(search)` finds available models. Create named collections with `group(name, fixtures)`; distribution does not create groups. `groups()` reads the saved collections. `render(view="front", t=0.0, width=960, height=540)` returns a headless image and `.path`, without moving the user's visualizer. Views: front, audience, overhead, quarter_left, quarter_right, dj. Quarter views are named from the audience: the audience's left is stage right (+u). Without `edit`, it renders the saved score. Pass `highlight=` a selection expression, or a list of fixtures and cells (`"<fixture>:<cell>"` or `(fixture, cell)`, cells from 0), to inspect them at full white with every other fixture dark and no score playing. `focus=` (the same things) frames just those from `view`'s side and turns past truss in the way; `camera=` places the camera outright, as `{"eye": (u, v, z), "target": (u, v, z), "fov": 50}` or a previous image's `.camera`, which `.orbit(right=deg, up=deg)` and `.zoom(factor)` move. Each render prints where it looked from and which way the picture is turned. `group(name, ...)` takes cells the same way; a library mode's `cells`, `colours` and `cell_colours` say what each cell is, and `mode.cells_with("white")` picks the cells that make a colour. The room uses its saved environment; `house=0` turns house lighting off for the preview. Highlight and draft-score previews are separate modes.
- `luma.perform` is the venue's Perform set, in the Perform tab's chats only: `describe()`, `patterns`, `pages`, `pattern(name)`. `add(graph, name=, selection=, at=(column, row))` adds a pattern in a cell, wiring a toggle pad (a `button` on the output's `alpha`, starting on the next beat) into a graph that has none; `button(..., start="bar")` makes a press land on the set's next bar; `p.pad` is its pad's control, or None; a live color is `control("main", kind="color")`, wired straight into `color=`; a speed is a fader into a clock, `time(every=curve(control("speed"), "Steps 2", low=1, high=2))`, which changes speed on the bar; a hit is a clock of presses, `k = time(on=button("pad", "flash", start="now"), duration=1)` with `alpha=curve(k, "Ramp down")`, each press its own fading event; `update`, `remove`, `reorder`, `add_page`, `update_page`, `place` (`"group"` with `group=`, a venue group to choose; `"list"` with `values=` of one kind, colors, numbers 0-1, or `"below"` (the color below, for a color list with no targets), and its fade, step and targets; `"fader"`; `"select"`, `"clear"`, `"master"`, `"tap"`), `move`, `update_fader`, `update_list`, `update_group` and `remove_cell` change it, and `busk()` adds a new page made from the venue's rig (groups, a base and an accent color list, effects, movement, hits, Select and Clear) beside the set, each checked and saved at once (a refusal raises `luma.PerformRefused`). Choose where, then act; nothing chosen means everything: `choose(*groups)` chooses groups, `play(p, level=)` and `stop(p)` turn a pad on there (on its own selection when none is chosen), `base(color, on=groups, fade=)` sets the base color (color only, lighting nothing; effects with blend `"below"` take it), `select(*patterns)` then `tap(list, index)` on a color list with no targets gives effects their own color (or `"below"` back), `clear()` chooses nothing and `clear(effects=True)` lets every effect go; `tap(cell, index)`, `next(cell)`, `set(control, value)` and `press(control)` audition, and `live()` shows what plays, where each effect plays, the groups chosen and `waiting`, each press waiting for its beat or bar. `bindings()`, `update_binding`, `unbind` and `learn(control, controller=)` (waits up to 20 s for hardware) manage MIDI and OSC. Controllers: `controllers()`, `ports()` (the MIDI ports open now), `add_controller(name, port=)`, `update_controller(c, name=, port=)`, `set_profile(c, profile)` (refused with why when it cannot be read or drawn), `light(c, (column, row) or address, "red" or (r, g, b), shine="steady"|"pulse"|"blink")`, `clear_lights(c)`, `map_page(c, page=)` and `remove_controller(c)`.
- Images are collected for you, never emitted by hand: every matplotlib figure still open at the end of the cell and every `luma.venue.render(...)` made during it comes back as an image. There is no `display()` — this is a plain Python process, not IPython.
