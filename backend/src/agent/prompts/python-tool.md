Orientation:
- `luma.catalog()` gives a compact overview. Drill into a relevant path, such as `luma.catalog("venue")` or `luma.catalog("features.drum_onsets")`; use `depth=` to see more of that branch. Request `depth=None` only when you need its full inventory.
- `edit = luma.track.edit()` captures the score document. A clip plays one form: `color@1`, `strobe.constant@1` or `aim@1`. Color accepts Time or Space; numeric inputs accept Time, Space, Random, Noise or Audio. Each Time or Random source owns events (`every`, optional `life`) or inherits them; a Time offset on Space makes a chase. Clip `fade` runs once over the clip. Place it with `edit.add_clip(form, beats=(32, 48), selection="front_wash", inputs=inputs)`, where `inputs` holds every input of the form; start from `luma.track.definition(form)["inputs"]` defaults. Use `bars=(1, 5)` or `seconds=(start, end)` when appropriate; all ranges are half-open. Beats are zero-based from the musical origin, bars one-based. `edit.update_clip(clip, inputs={...})` changes values; a `None` value restores the form default. `edit.remove_clip(clip)` deletes it. A selection always lights its whole group; for a random share use Random on brightness; for a pulse use Time on brightness with `events.every` in beats. Transition Aim positions with clip fades; layer motion with Offset.
- Layers combine forms through blend modes; there are no custom graphs. `edit.source()` is the canonical score JSON; `edit.replace_source(source)` stages it. Native editing, Python and model-authored JSON use one validator.
- Verify with `edit.diff()`, `edit.check()` and `view = edit.window(...)`. `view.timeline()` plots clips; `view.output.heatmap()` renders the actual candidate. Inspect a proposed clip in 3D with `luma.venue.render(edit=edit, only=clip, t=...)`, then omit `only` to inspect the full draft and its blends at representative timestamps. `edit.apply()` writes the whole candidate over the score, including its unchanged clips; open the edit right before you start changing things and apply promptly, since a change landed elsewhere meanwhile is not merged in and will be overwritten. In a child workspace it advances only the detached score, which a supervisor later merges.
- `luma.music` is the track as heard. `luma.music.feel` gives the felt tempo against the grid (often 2x). `listen(bars)`, `deviations(bars)`, `modulation(bars)`, `similar(at)` and `sections()` return compact text; `onsets`, `mert`, `envelopes` and `beat_features` hold the arrays behind them. Positions are `bar.beat.16th`: UI bar from 1, UI beat from 0, felt 16th; quote them as printed. n2n hat onsets are low-confidence.
- `luma.audio` is signal: `vocals`, `rest` (the mix without vocals) and `mix` for reference. `luma.features` is what was derived from audio (beats, downbeats, drum onsets, bar classifications, chords, waveform bands, MERT). Pick the branch that matches the question.
- Tensors expose `.values` (numpy), `.shape`, `.axes`, `.times_s`, `.unit`, `.provenance`. Keyed families are dict-style: `luma.features.drum_onsets["kick"]`, `luma.music.onsets["kick"]`.
- `luma.venue` is the room. Plan coordinates are `u` (stage right), `v` (toward the audience), and height `z`, in metres; angles are degrees. Use `describe()` for a compact live overview and `nodes()` for placed geometry. `fixtures` is the patch; `fixture_library(search)` finds available models. Create named collections with `group(name, fixtures)`; distribution does not create groups. `groups()` reads the saved collections. `render(view="front", t=0.0, width=960, height=540)` returns a headless image and `.path`, without moving the user's visualizer. Views: front, audience, overhead, quarter_left, quarter_right, dj. Without `edit`, it renders the saved score. Pass `highlight="<selection expression>"` to inspect a group at full white with every other fixture dark and no score playing. The room uses its saved environment; `house=0` turns house lighting off for the preview. Highlight and draft-score previews are separate modes.
- Images are collected for you, never emitted by hand: every matplotlib figure still open at the end of the cell and every `luma.venue.render(...)` made during it comes back as an image. There is no `display()` — this is a plain Python process, not IPython.
