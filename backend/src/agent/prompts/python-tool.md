Execute Python in a namespace that persists for this agent thread. Current Luma state lives under `luma` and is refreshed before every call; the variables, functions and imports you create persist across calls. numpy, scipy, librosa and matplotlib are available. You get back stdout, stderr, the last expression's value, a traceback when it fails, and any matplotlib figures as images you can actually see. Write normal cell-shaped Python — no wrapper function, no `return`.

For every call, name the cell's work before its code. Choose a verb that fits the work, and give it in two forms: `verb` ends in -ing and `verbPast` is its past tense, for example “Measuring” and “Measured”. `purpose` is a short phrase that follows the verb, for example “beat energy in bars 7–13”. Do not restate the code.

Orientation:
- `luma.catalog()` gives a compact overview. Drill into a relevant path, such as `luma.catalog("venue")` or `luma.catalog("features.drum_onsets")`; use `depth=` to see more of that branch. Request `depth=None` only when you need its full inventory.
- `edit = luma.track.edit()` captures the score document. A clip has a name, a time range, a selection, a blend mode, a seed and one graph. The builders are bare names in every cell: `time`, `space`, `noise`, `audio`, `curve`, `mirror`, `shuffle`, `group`, `split`, `max`, `min`, and the outputs `color`, `aim`, `strobe`; `preset("Chase")` copies a shipped clip. Coordinates (`time`, `space`, `noise`, `audio`) go through `curve` into an input: `color(brightness=curve(time(every=1), "Drop"))`. Curves combine with `*`, `+`, `-`, `max()` and `min()`: `brightness=cut * fade` (a list is an error). `alpha` on `color` and `strobe` is the clip's opacity. Values are plain: numbers, `(u, v, z)`, `(r, g, b)` or `"#RRGGBB"`, curve and gradient preset names. The same variable wired twice is one node. Place with `edit.add_clip(graph, name="Pulse", at=("9", "13"), selection="front_wash")`, or use `bars=(9, 13)` or `seconds=(start, end)`. A position is `bar.beat.16th`, all three from 1, as in the editor; `"9"` is `9.1.1`. Every range includes its start and excludes its end. `clip.at` gives a clip's range as positions. `luma.track.position_at(seconds)` and `luma.track.seconds_at(position)` convert between time and position. A tuple color is linear Rec. 2020; `luma.track.from_srgb("#ff8000")` converts sRGB. `add_clip` and `edit.update_clip(clip, graph=..., name=...)` check that clip at once and raise `ClipError` with the node, the input and an example fix. `edit.remove_clip(clip)` deletes it. `clip.graph.source()` prints Python that rebuilds a clip's graph. A selection always lights its whole group.
- Layers combine clips through blend modes. `edit.source()` is the canonical score JSON; `edit.replace_source(source)` stages it. Native editing, Python and model-authored JSON use one checker.
- Verify with `edit.diff()`, `edit.check()` and `view = edit.window(...)`. `edit.diff()` prints a short plan, one line per changed clip; `edit.diff(full=True)` gives the raw clips. `view.timeline()` plots clips; `view.output.heatmap()` renders the actual candidate; `view.output.values` is `[primitive, time, rgb]`, `view.output.aim.values` / `.aim.weight` the aim and `view.output.strobe.values` the shutter. Inspect a proposed clip in 3D with `luma.venue.render(edit=edit, only=clip, t=...)`, then omit `only` to inspect the full draft and its blends at representative timestamps. `edit.apply()` writes the whole candidate over the score, including its unchanged clips; open the edit right before you start changing things and apply promptly, since a change landed elsewhere meanwhile is not merged in and will be overwritten. In a child workspace it advances only the detached score, which a supervisor later merges.
- `luma.music` is the track as heard. `luma.music.feel` gives the felt tempo against the grid (often 2x). `listen(bars)`, `deviations(bars)`, `modulation(bars)`, `similar(at)` and `sections()` return compact text; `onsets`, `mert`, `envelopes` and `beat_features` hold the arrays behind them. Its positions are `bar.beat.16th` too; quote them as printed. At 2x feel a felt 16th is half a 16th, such as `13.2.3.5`. n2n hat onsets are low-confidence.
- `luma.audio` is signal: `vocals`, `rest` (the mix without vocals) and `mix` for reference. `luma.features` is what was derived from audio (beats, downbeats, drum onsets, bar classifications, chords, waveform bands, MERT). Pick the branch that matches the question.
- Tensors expose `.values` (numpy), `.shape`, `.axes`, `.times_s`, `.unit`, `.provenance`. Keyed families are dict-style: `luma.features.drum_onsets["kick"]`, `luma.music.onsets["kick"]`.
- `luma.venue` is the room. Plan coordinates are `u` (stage right), `v` (toward the audience), and height `z`, in metres; angles are degrees. Use `describe()` for a compact live overview and `nodes()` for placed geometry. `fixtures` is the patch; `fixture_library(search)` finds available models. Create named collections with `group(name, fixtures)`; distribution does not create groups. `groups()` reads the saved collections. `render(view="front", t=0.0, width=960, height=540)` returns a headless image and `.path`, without moving the user's visualizer. Views: front, audience, overhead, quarter_left, quarter_right, dj. Quarter views are named from the audience: the audience's left is stage right (+u). Without `edit`, it renders the saved score. Pass `highlight="<selection expression>"` to inspect a group at full white with every other fixture dark and no score playing. The room uses its saved environment; `house=0` turns house lighting off for the preview. Highlight and draft-score previews are separate modes.
- Images are collected for you, never emitted by hand: every matplotlib figure still open at the end of the cell and every `luma.venue.render(...)` made during it comes back as an image. There is no `display()` — this is a plain Python process, not IPython.
