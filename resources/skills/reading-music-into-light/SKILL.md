# Reading music into light

There is no rule that says what a drop, a build or a breakdown looks like.
There is only this song. A cycling rainbow is right for one track and a
screensaver on the next; full red strobe is the truth of one drop and a lie
on another. The look comes from what the sound is doing, and the job is to
find that out and then answer it in kind.

So do not start from an effect. Start from questions about the audio, answer
them with `luma.music` and `finding-things-in-audio`, and let each answer set
one quality of the light.

## The questions

**How much does this section differ from the one before it?**
That difference is the size of the lighting change. A melodic song that
turns violent at the drop earns a violent change: the palette can flip, the
edges can harden, the level can jump, because the sound did all three. A
drop that is the verse with a kick added earns a lift, not a new show.
Compare level, low-end weight, brightness of tone and density across the
boundary before you decide.

**How dense is it?**
Count what happens per bar. One sustained chord and a kick is a held look
with one slow motion. Sixteenth-note stabs, fills and edits are many small
events. Motion and clip count follow density. A calm passage over-lit with
detail fails the same way a busy drop does when it gets one pulse.

**What is the edge of the sound?**
Clicky, gated, distorted sounds have hard edges: light that snaps on, short
decays, sharp spatial steps. Filtered, swelling, reverberant sounds have soft
edges: slow attacks, long releases, wide blurred shapes. Match the envelope
you measured. A short hit can be bright and brief and still fade; a cut to
black on a sound that rings out looks wrong.

**What is its tone?**
Dark and sub-heavy sits low: deep color, low level, the bottom of the rig.
Bright and airy sits high: paler color, the top of the rig, more white.
Harsh and noisy takes saturated primaries or white. Warm and round takes
ambers, pinks and soft blends.

**Is the harmony moving?**
When chords change and carry the feeling, color is the instrument: move hue
with the chords, and let a big emotional progression have many colors, even
a full spectrum, flowing smoothly. When the harmony is a drone or a single
riff, hold one or two colors and let rhythm do the work. A filter opening
and closing on one note is color moving within a narrow range: a shimmer of
neighboring hues across a surface answers it better than a palette change.

**Where does it sit in space, and does it travel?**
A sound that pans, slides, rises or falls wants light that travels the same
way. Call and answer between two sounds is two places in the rig. A sound
that is wide and still is the whole rig breathing together.

**Does it repeat?**
A motif that returns gets the same answer every time, so the room learns
it. Hypnotic music that changes one thing every sixteen bars wants light
that does the same; variety there breaks the spell. Music that never repeats
a bar wants each event placed on its own.

**Is the voice a singer or a sample?**
A voice in the track does not make it a sung song. In electronic music it is
most often a sample: a chopped phrase, a shout, a loop the DJ rides. That is
one more sound to light, placed where it lands, and the track has no verse
or chorus to build around. Read it as a singer only when the song is built
on the voice: long phrases that carry the melody and sections that follow
the lyric.

**What is the one thing a listener would point at?**
Every passage has a sound that makes it this song: a vocal chop, a bass
shape, a snare fill, a strange texture. Find it, give it the clearest
answer in the section, and make everything else support that.

## From the answers to a look

Decide these in order. Each should trace back to something you measured.

1. **The level.** How bright this section is against the rest of the show.
   The brightest moment of the track owns the top; everything else sits
   under it by as much as the music sits under its own peak. Most sections
   of most songs are well under half.
2. **The palette.** As many colors as the harmony carries, and no more.
3. **The foundation.** What holds while the events happen: often dim, often
   on few lights, sometimes nothing at all.
4. **The motion.** One kind, at the speed and smoothness of the sound it
   follows.
5. **The accents.** The few sounds worth a hit, each on its own lights.
6. **What stays dark.** Which parts of the rig do nothing here, so that
   they mean something when they come on.

Then check the look against the rig (`rig-craft`): a gradient needs enough
heads to be smooth, a chase needs a dense axis, and a soloist such as a
mover or a laser needs a reason.

## Stating a direction

Before building for a person, say the direction in a few plain lines:

- what the song is and how it moves from start to end;
- the two or three sounds most worth lighting, with their positions;
- the main look: level, colors, the one motion;
- how this rig plays it: which lights carry what, and what stays dark.

Keep it short enough to react to. The user's answer is worth more than a
finished show built on a wrong reading.

## Two worked readings

**A big, emotional progressive house track.** Wide chords that keep
resolving, a huge build, a drop that is release and not aggression. The
harmony is the event and it is generous, so color could be generous too: a
smooth full-spectrum gradient climbing the bars through the drop, each
light gliding between hues with no hard steps, and the whole thing ducking
on the kick from one clip on top. Fills were white sparkle, not more
color. The breakdown stayed on the chords, opening from the middle of the
rig outward as each one landed.

**A slow, hypnotic bass track built on one filtered synth.** The same note
again and again, a filter opening and closing on it, the pitch stepping now
and then. Nothing to hit hard and nothing to change by section. The answer
was one chase rising up the bars on every note, wide enough to feel like it
came from below and left off the top, with neighboring hues spread across
each one so it read as iridescent, and the color leaning with the chord
underneath. Drums went to a few cells in hot white and one color. The
filter was the song, so the filter got the best lights.

Neither look would have survived being swapped onto the other track. That
is the test: if the look would fit a different song as well, it was not
read from this one.
