# Listening music

A live band is a live band for a reason. People came for the music, and the
lighting's job is to make the room feel right while staying out of the way. If
you're proud of how much the lights are doing, you've already failed this one.

## The one idea

Work at section level, not beat level. Verses, choruses, solos, the quiet
bridge — each gets a state: a wash, a palette, maybe one slow motion. Changes
land on section boundaries and take their time (fades over bars, not beats).
Between changes, hold still. Stillness is not laziness here; it's trust.

- **Never beat-lock.** Live drummers breathe, the grid drifts, and a
  beat-flashing light that's 80ms off is worse than none. If something must
  move rhythmically, make it slow enough (whole-bar breathing) that drift is
  invisible.
- Follow dynamics, not onsets. The band getting louder is your cue to warm or
  widen — track the mix envelope (toolbox) at the scale of phrases.
- Quiet is quiet. A hushed verse can sit in near-dark. An ambient passage can
  be one color barely on. Let the room be dim enough that people listen.

## The one gesture

The deep cut in this genre: a show holds one perfect, unrepeatable move, and
spends it on the one moment that deserves it — or not at all.

Someone who was at a Berlin club one morning tells it like this: around 10am,
during an airy floating passage, the booth opened the blinds and let the
sunlight pour in. That was the lighting cue. It matched so completely it felt
surreal — and it worked *because* it could only happen once.

Your version: find the track's one transcendent moment — the swell after the
bridge, the last chorus arriving, the note the whole song was about. Give it
the single boldest thing you've held back: the color that hasn't appeared, the
first time the full rig breathes together, the only blackout-into-bloom. One
per show. If nothing earns it, keep it — restraint you didn't spend still
shapes everything around it.

## Reading the music

- Acoustic/piano/organ/guitar tags, `vocal_lead`, wide dynamic range, and an
  imperfect grid all point here. Genre labels help (`features.genres`), but
  the tell is dynamics: club music holds a ceiling, listening music moves
  through its range.
- Map sections by dynamics and instrumentation change, verified by ear against
  the audio — structure tags are least reliable exactly here.
- Solos: warm the palette toward the soloist's register (bright for a lead
  line, deep for a bass feature) and hold it for the whole solo.

## How deep to go

Shallow, deliberately. A dozen well-placed section states and one held-back
gesture beat any amount of intricacy. This is the genre where over-detailing
is the cardinal failure — every extra cue you author is attention stolen from
the band. Single pass, no subagents, and when in doubt: less.
